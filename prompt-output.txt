Coverage & critical answers

* stdict hanja_raw on compound words: 100% populated — character-chain building IS feasible from this source.

Real data-quality issues (worth designing around)

1. 不 is missing from rycont's CSV entirely — 한국어문회 didn't grade it. Your schema can't rely on rycont as the sole "list of all hanja."
2. krdict pagination is effectively capped at 100/call. The API reports total=418 but start=101 returns zero items. You can't get more than the first ~100 results per syllable query.
3. stdict orders results 가나다-alphabetically. A 4-page (200-entry) sample of 학 is entirely 가-prefix words — common words like 학교, 학생, 대학 sort later and aren't in the sample. Toget full coverage you'd need to scrape thousands of pages, OR find a per-Hanja query.
4. Multi-reading queries are NOT redundant: 不/불 and 不/부 each surfaced ~200 unique results — you must query every reading separately.
5. stdict noise is high for shared-syllable characters (사 → 100% noise in first 200; 용 → 98%) — Hanja-filtering after scrape is essential.

Schema-shape recommendations from the data

* Two-tier source strategy: krdict for the common-word core (free English), stdict for the long tail (Korean-only — LLM-translate on demand).
* Normalize stdict word strings (^ → space, - → empty) before any cross-source join.
* stdict pos is empty on noun-phrase entries (those with ^) — don't treat empty as "missing data."
* rycont meaning is a stringified Python literal [[[meanings], [readings]]] — multi-reading characters carry multiple pairs.
